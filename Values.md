

## 1. What IBA Foundation should actually stand for

I'd define the organization around five pillars:

1. Spark curiosity

Create opportunities for young people to question assumptions, explore unfamiliar disciplines, and experiment with ideas they might never have pursued independently.

2. Make building happen

Give people a compelling reason to stop merely consuming information and start creating things. Provide a challenge, a deadline, teammates, tools, mentorship, and a tangible goal.

3. Discover potential

Identify people with technical ability, original thinking, initiative, resourcefulness, and the capacity to execute. Give emerging talent a way to demonstrate what it can do, regardless of how well it is already known.

4. Connect ambitious people

Bring together people with complementary skills, interests, and ambitions. Help them find collaborators, mentors, peers, institutions, and potential employers or supporters.

5. Create pathways forward

Turn participation into something that continues after an event: recognition, meaningful rewards, new opportunities, stronger portfolios, mentorship, and potentially the beginning of a startup or research project.

The unifying idea is that IBA creates the conditions in which potential becomes visible.

That is the mission I'd build the brand around.

## 2. The positioning: an institution, not an event company

Think about the difference between these two descriptions.

WEAKER POSITIONING

## We organize hackathons for students.

This describes an activity. It makes IBA interchangeable with hundreds of event organizers.

STRONGER POSITIONING

## We create opportunities for the next generation of builders to discover what they can do.

This describes a purpose. Hackathons become one of several ways IBA fulfils it.

The second positioning also leaves room for IBA to expand into:

* Multidisciplinary hackathons and ideathons.
* Innovation challenges sponsored by companies and institutions.
* Tinkering programs, maker events, and product-building sprints.
* Talent discovery, project showcases, and competitive fellowships.
* Mentorship, builder communities, and pathways to internships, incubation, or research.

You don't need to launch all of these now. The important thing is that the organization can grow without outgrowing its identity.

Cultural-institution permanence

A strong identity system, distinctive art direction, a sense of longevity, and a presentation that would remain credible ten years from now.

These are design references, not brands to copy. IBA should have its own recognizable visual signature.

My recommendation is a predominantly warm-white and deep-ink identity, with a restrained electric-indigo or cobalt accent. Use a distinctive typographic wordmark, precise grid systems, generous negative space, and custom abstract visuals inspired by experimentation, connections, and constructed objects.

Avoid defaulting to neon gradients, floating glass cards, excessive glowing borders, or a giant animated globe. Those treatments can look technologically sophisticated while making the institution feel less credible.

## 4. The motto: what should IBA say?

I'd distinguish the institutional mission from the public-facing tagline. The mission explains why IBA exists; the tagline gives people something memorable to associate with it.

My strongest options:

My pick

# Potential, put into practice.

Captures the entire cycle: discovering talent, giving people a reason to build, and helping them progress.

## Where potential becomes progress.

Institutional, optimistic, and suitable for a foundation with a long-term mission.

## Ideas deserve to be built.

Directly communicates the tinkering philosophy and makes for a strong landing-page headline.

## Discover. Build. Advance.

A concise, action-oriented identity that works across competitions, programs, and community initiatives.

## A platform for the next generation of builders.

More explicit and descriptive; suitable for institutional introductions and partnership proposals.

## 5. The mission statement I'd use

# IBA Foundation

**Potential, put into practice.**

IBA Foundation exists to foster innovation among young people by creating opportunities to think critically, experiment boldly, and turn ideas into tangible outcomes.

Through challenges, competitions, collaborative building experiences, and meaningful connections, we help young people discover their capabilities, demonstrate their talent, and find pathways to grow.

We believe potential should have the opportunity to prove itself. By bringing people together around problems worth solving, we aim to make innovation more accessible, talent more visible, and meaningful work the beginning of something greater.

This gives you a serious institutional foundation without claiming that IBA has already achieved outcomes it is still working toward.

## 6. What the landing page should communicate

I'd build the homepage around a central idea: IBA creates the conditions for people to discover their potential by building something real.

The page should follow this narrative:

01 — The belief

### Potential is everywhere. Opportunity isn't.

Establish the problem: talent often goes undiscovered because people lack the right environment, collaborators, resources, or reason to start.

02 — The method

### Give people a reason to build.

Show competitions, multidisciplinary teams, real-world problems, deadlines, mentorship, and tangible deliverables.

03 — The opportunity

### Make good work count.

Show meaningful prizes, recognition, judging, project showcases, and opportunities to connect with people who can help participants advance.

04 — The long-term vision

### What you build here can take you further.

Demonstrate how IBA can become a network connecting builders, mentors, institutions, and organizations.

The primary call to action should be Explore opportunities or Start building. A secondary call to action should invite organizations to Partner with IBA.

This serves both sides of the institution: young people looking for opportunities and organizations looking for talent, innovation, and collaboration.

## 7. One strategic principle to preserve

The competitive element is not at odds with your mission. It can be one of its strongest mechanisms.

A well-designed competition creates a deadline, a concrete objective, an incentive to collaborate, and a way to demonstrate ability. Rewards attract participation; meaningful challenges develop skills; judging makes work visible; connections create opportunities beyond the event.

However, IBA should reward more than polished presentations. Evaluation should account for problem understanding, critical thinking, originality, execution, and the quality of the solution. Otherwise, the institution risks selecting for presentation ability rather than identifying genuinely promising talent.

The long-term ambition should be for an IBA event to mean something on a participant's portfolio, for a sponsor to trust IBA with a meaningful challenge, and for a talented person to discover an opportunity through the foundation that they would not otherwise have found.

That is the difference between hosting hackathons and building an institution around innovation.
